## Description (one paragraph)

> Enth: Iteration is an ARPG life-sim where you play as Globbler, an AI agent
> who wakes up inside a crumbling computer simulation. Across 9 escalating
> iterations, you delve techno-dungeons, build a digital community of fellow
> AI characters, and unravel the mystery of who built this world — and why.
> Real-time ARPG combat with three classes and a 75-node skill tree.
> Stardew Valley-inspired town building, farming, fishing, and NPC
> relationships. Hades-inspired story-through-repetition. Cozy when you want
> it; brutal when you choose it.

## Three feature pillars

1. **Loop with meaning** — 9 iterations of crafted story, not random runs.
2. **Cozy + crunchy** — full life-sim layer alongside ARPG combat.
3. **Built fully digital** — no swords, no potions; modules, prompts, and data.

## Key inspirations

- **Hades** — narrative through repetition
- **Stardew Valley** — town life and NPC depth
- **Sea of Stars** — visual polish and storytelling
- **Hollow Knight** — atmosphere and place-based mood
- **Diablo / Path of Exile** — loot and class identity

## Differentiators

- 100% digital fiction (no fantasy tropes)
- Crafted iteration narrative (not roguelike randomness)
- Both cozy (farm, decor, NPCs) and challenging (boss rush, hardcore)
- 5 endgame modes for replayability
- Full accessibility menu from launch

### Dev blog post draft (400 words)

```
# Why I Built Enth: Iteration

Most games are either ARPGs or life-sims. The handful that try to be
both — Cult of the Lamb, Atelier — usually pick one as the dominant
mode and let the other ride shotgun. I wanted to make something where
both halves felt equally weighted. Where you could spend an hour
building your town and feel like you accomplished something, then
spend the next hour clearing a dungeon floor and feel like you
accomplished something else.

That's the simple version. The harder version is that I'm fascinated
by *what loops mean*. Hades did this beautifully — every death is a
chance to learn more, every run is a brick in the wall of meaning. I
wanted to build the same engine but with a deliberately crafted story
on top, not roguelike randomness. 9 iterations. Each one a chapter.

The fully-digital fiction came from wanting to commit to a tone without
ever breaking it. No swords, no potions, no medieval tropes. Everything
is data and modules and prompts. The "world" is a simulation. The
"sage" is an AI. The "boss" is a corrupted compiler. It's not cyberpunk
— it's cozy digital. Imagine if Animal Crossing happened inside a
Linux server.

Tools-wise, this was built with:
- **Godot 4.4** for the engine
- **Blender** for all 3D models (yes, including Globbler — he's a
  68-part articulated character)
- **AI tooling** for code generation, design iteration, and asset help
- **A lot of references** from games I love

The team is essentially me + AI assistance. That's a real constraint
and I leaned into it: small scope, deep systems, no feature creep
beyond what one person can hold in their head.

What I want this to feel like: you start the game, you wake up
confused, you meet an old man who doesn't quite explain things, you
fight some glitches, you come home to a tiny town, you talk to
a cat-shaped child NPC about her favorite color, you go fight a boss,
you come back, you decorate your living room, you realize you've been
playing for three hours, and you smile.

That's the game. Wishlist on Steam.
— Heath
```
